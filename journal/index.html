Game Design Research

The thought that stayed with me throughout reading of Dori's Game Design UX Best Practices wasn't so much the advice given by the author but rather the way he threw around terms like "dark UX" as though it were nothing. In this instance, the act of positioning buttons in areas where they can be hit by accident is seen as a legitimate business strategy. This is the case even when it is discussed in the same light as other design best practices.

That said, quite a few of the concepts presented in the article are actually useful. Proper button sizes, smooth onboarding processes, and decreasing friction are all examples of things that fit in very well with our interactive design principles learned in class. However, while these ideas are definitely helpful, the addition of reward psychology and business interests is something we haven't necessarily discussed yet. Overall, the article provides excellent tips on UI game design and understands user interaction.

Visual Thinking Strategies Research

Reflection of Reading 10 Intriguing Photographs to Teach Close Reading and Visual Thinking Skills helped me understand that I do not really give myself a chance to truly observe anything before deciding. The way the author uses the layered questions technique, by dividing observation and analysis, seems super interesting to me and will definitely be used more in my design practice.

For this exercise, I decided to analyze an agency website called Locomotive , which has been awarded the Awwwards prize. What I have found really interesting is the way scrolling works on the website. Nothing appears suddenly, but there is always some logic to how and when new elements appear. The fonts are clear and bold, while the negative space is well-balanced.

However, what I see as a drawback is the heavy use of scrolljacking, which might cause people discomfort and could be overstimulating. Overall, this is an excellent example of a design that requires certain intentions to work.

Overlays Design Pattern Research

The article about best practices with regard to modals and overlays has brought attention to the problem of misuse. The author explains which situations call for an overlay: those in which the user needs a little extra focus on some actions within the application context. Also, the author provides criteria for determining whether it is possible to say that an overlay is no good in such cases.

Some of the tips were interesting and helpful. In particular, I would like to mention one thing that was noted by the author: there should always be a possibility of closing the overlay. Moreover, modals have to be activated manually because they interrupt user actions anyway. Finally, the information contained within an overlay has to be limited to something brief, and the need for scrolling indicates the improper use of the pattern.

I believe that in interactive media design, it will be necessary to pay close attention to the proper use of overlays. They are used widely in various situations.

Best Practices for Form Design Reflection

The article emphasizes the effectiveness of form design to improve user experience and increase conversion rates. One key idea that stood out to me was the importance of minimizing input fields. To reduce cognitive load, it's best to only ask for necessary information. This supports the idea that in some cases, less is more.

Another concept I found interesting was structuring forms in a way that supports user flow. For example, single columns and breaking long forms into multiple steps. It’s important to understand how users process information.

Lastly, ensuring forms are accessible is important. This highlights the ethical responsibilities of a designer. Making sure that forms are readable and inclusive improves usability. In conclusion, the article demonstrates that thoughtful user-centered design can benefit both the user and the business.
